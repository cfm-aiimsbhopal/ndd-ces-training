Quick reference

Short versions of Manual 1, for use in the field.

Your day in one cluster
  1. Before 8 am: phones charged, connect once so forms update, kit packed (cards, chalk, slips, sample tablet, letters)
  2. Arrive: meet the ASHA or AWW as a guide; confirm the village or ward on your list
  3. Segment (F4): sketch map, make the planned number of segments, the guide draws one
  4. Select: number every household in the segment, toss a coin, follow List A or B
  5. Interview: every household on the list; revisit absentees later the same day
  6. Verify (F10): one government school and one AWC, after the interviews
  7. Close: finish F4, send all forms, message your nodal officer
Segmenting the cluster
UnitWhat to do
Up to 600 householdsF4 shows the planned number of segments (about 50 households each). Divide the map into exactly that many parts with clear boundaries; number them; the guide draws one slip.
Over 600 households (24 clusters, mostly town wards)Step 1: divide into the number of parts F4 gives, along roads or mohallas; draw one part. Step 2: estimate its households with the ASHA; F4 gives the segments; draw one.
Very small unitSurvey every household. If F4 asks, add the nearest village or ward and record it.
Never change the number of segments or parts on your own. Unequal segments are fine; gaps, overlaps or a skipped hamlet are not.
List A and List B
  1. Number every household in the drawn segment with chalk, 1, 2, 3 … along one route. A household = people who share a kitchen, not a building.
  2. Toss a coin in front of the guide: heads List A, tails List B. Record it in F4 and the household form.
  3. Visit every coloured number on the card, up to the last household numbered. The form refuses a grey number.

The household interview: key questions

H5 probe (read out first)

"कृपया एक वर्ष से बड़े बच्चों, रिश्तेदारों के यहाँ रह रहे बच्चों, और छात्रावास में रहने वाले पर यहीं के सामान्य निवासी बच्चों को भी शामिल करें।"

H5a All usual residents during 6–9 October, including those away today · H5b Of them, children aged 1–19 (babies under 1 are not counted)

C5 (show the sample albendazole tablet)

कृमि मुक्ति के दिनों (6–9 अक्टूबर) में, क्या [नाम] को स्कूल, आंगनवाड़ी या कहीं और यह गोली दी गई थी?

C6

क्या [नाम] ने गोली खाई (चबाकर, पीसकर या पूरी निगलकर)?
Who answersRule
Child 10 years or older, at homeThe child answers, after assent (up to age 17)
Child under 10Parent or main caregiver
Child awayRevisit or phone; otherwise an adult of the household
Nobody can answerOnly after a revisit or phone call; tell the nodal officer
Interview at the household only, without teachers, AWWs or ASHAs listening. Never at school.
Record verification (F10)
  • Sites: the government school most sampled children attend, and the AWC serving the segment
  • Both team members recount the ticks independently
  • Reported figure missing: choose “NA / data not received”; never enter 0
  • Figure reported but no ticked register: record it; the form marks it “not verifiable”
  • Photograph the reporting form only, never registers or children’s names
  • VF outside 0.90–1.10: ask and record the explanation; do not argue or correct anything
Golden rules
  1. Never replace a cluster, segment or household
  2. Draw segments and the list by chance, in front of the guide
  3. Number every household before selecting
  4. Interview alone: no worker listening
  5. Show the tablet at every child’s C5
  6. Send every evening; never delete sent forms

Frequently asked questions

Can I use an iPhone?
No. ODK Collect runs only on Android. Use an Android phone for the survey.
Will I see new versions of the forms automatically?
Yes. Your team code keeps the forms matched to the server. Connect to the internet each morning before going to the field.
Where do I get the list of villages for my team?
From your nodal officer, a day before each visit. The village list is never published, to keep the survey independent.
The segment has many more households than 50. Should I make more segments?
No. Keep the planned number. A bigger segment means more households on the list; that is expected because villages have grown since 2011. Call your nodal officer if a segment has more than about 120 households.
A household has no child aged 1–19. Do I still visit it?
Yes, if it is on your list. The form ends quickly with result code 2. It is part of the sample.
Can the ASHA or AWW stay during the interview?
No. They guide you round and introduce you at the door, then wait away from the house.
